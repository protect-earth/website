It starts here!

The long-term vision for the new planting is to create areas of mixed and native woodland that complement existing natural environmental, landscape and historical features, and take into account a changing climate for future generations to enjoy.

The new woodland will contain a diverse range of native and non-native species planted in contrasting areas and patterns to create a mosaic of habitats and wooded features that will provide both native woodland habitat that will encourage wildlife and a landscape that contains a variety of trees from different continents mirroring the Victorian heritage of the site.

The native woods will be managed to become fully established within 10 years and achieve a closed canopy within 15 years. Hardy native pioneer species including birch, rowan, aspen and alder and will form the main tree canopy along with oak, small leaved lime and wild cherry planted within this mixture for diversity and to provide a long lived woodland.  A wide range of native shrubs typical of mixed lowland broadleaved woodland will be planted up to 40% of the mixture as source of food for wildlife and for general diversity. The woodland may be managed in subsequent years to ensure the feature is preserved and allowed to mature providing a local amenity as well as natural habitat.

We were lucky to be able to plant the trees before the snow came, but our photos have a very winter feel!

We returned in April when the weather was better to lay some biodegradable mulch mats to supress the grass around the trees. We were pleased to see that our saplings were beginning to sprout.
