Our team were pleased to see that almost all the surviving trees are in excellent condition and displaying robust growth.  It is clear that the landowner has worked hard to maintain the trees, by weeding between trees and within guards, and straightening and replacing guards and stakes.

There is a slightly lower survival rate and more frequent stunting in the plot's centre, while the trees closer to each edge are generally highly vigorous and large. One small patch of low survival  was directly under a large oak canopy - presumably having been shaded out by the larger tree.

There is some evidence of deer presence on-site, but the landowner reports it to not be a significant threat to the saplings.

The meadow planting has been unsuccessful, despite trying a yellow-rattle seed mix, exactly the same type of meadow failure as at Pantpurlais. Our team spotted one Himalayan balsam plant on site which we removed immediately. It has largely been eliminated here, but neighbouring properties still have pockets of it on their land.

There are a mix of plants occurring naturally across the site with plenty of common sneezewort, sharp rush, bird's foot trefoil, and lesser stitchwort, as well as some smaller areas of ragwort, thistle and sorrel.
