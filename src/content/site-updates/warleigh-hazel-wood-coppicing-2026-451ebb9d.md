Adjacent to the fields of Warleigh Nature Reserve, Protect Earth has taken in 11 acres of ancient woodland to restore and manage. Named Hazel Wood, it contains, amongst other very exciting things, an area of hazel coppice which has become overgrown during the last 30 years or so, which makes this section dark and less biodiverse. To encourage a more diverse growth of species and canopy levels we decided to make a start and coppice as much as we could before the end of February, the end of the coppicing season. Fun fact: we’re told it's named Hazel Wood not for the species within, but for a previous owner named Hazel.

Phil and the team spent the day diligently training with a coppicing expert, then, invited several groups of volunteers to come and learn how to coppice too. The volunteers used handsaws, and in between volunteer days those members of the team qualified to use chainsaws tackled the larger trunks.

As you can see, the result was a beautiful, bright clearing which will allow more biodiversity to emerge. So far, the harvest of hazel has been put to good use to create steps for the compost toilet and posts for the deer fencing around the clearing to prevent deer nibbling the tender new shoots.
