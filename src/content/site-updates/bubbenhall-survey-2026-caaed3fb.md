The woodland here is doing fairly well, especially the trees planted south of the river, with 78% surviving. Those north of the river near the established tree cover were smaller but this is to be expected due to the lower light access. 63% of those sampled here had survived.

The hedgerow tells a very different story. The hedgerow to the north has been completely swallowed by scrub, brambles and mature tree canopy and was undetectable. As we were unable to sample this part of the hedgerow we are classifying this as 0% survival for now, although it is likely that some of the saplings will have survived. The eastern hedgerow was in very good condition apart from a few small dead patches along its length, with an 82% survival rate. In general, the trees in this part of the hedgerow were showing vigorous growth.

The wildflower meadow is dominated by borage, with occasional thistle, marigold, and common lambsquarters. These plants are dotted around the area between patches of completely bare ground.
