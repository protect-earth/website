When we went to carry out our survey this August we found that the survival rates here have been very varied, and lower than we would have hoped, despite the recent restock. The landowners have done their best by weeding between the trees this year.

Most of the new trees had shown no signs of grown, probably because they were planted towards the end of the season. It would appear that only 12 of the 40 older saplings sampled  in this area were still alive.

The shelterbelt has fared much better with 57 alive out of the 84 sampled, despite  the ferns growing vigorously along part of its length. The surviving trees are now tall and reasonably healthy, with the birches doing particularly well.

The woodland has fared best of all with 126 out of 150 were alive and well. Some of them would have been protected from the worst of the summer heat by more mature trees. There were dense brambles in the centre of the woodland but this does not appear to be a problem at the moment.
