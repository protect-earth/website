We planted here on the banks of the River Wharfe near Burnsall, North Yorkshire. There are many benefits gained by establishing riparian trees, including: the reduction of soil erosion; the creation of a buffer zone against the leaching and surface run-off of pollution and nutrients; providing shade and maintaining oxygen levels during periods of low flow; as well as creating shelter and feeding opportunities for fish. However, the main aim of this project is to stabilise three distinct sections of  the ‘Higher Bank’ – an ‘effectively linear’ 200m section. ‘Middle Grassy Bank’ – an area of 840 m2. And, ‘Sand and Gravel Bank Area’ – an area of 2267 m2 at river level.

Andy, our trustee based in Scotland, was nearest so he came down to lead the planting. As this was a mid-spring planting, it was decided to use irrigation to give the saplings the best start.

The ground was quite boggy in places, and very uneven, making this a difficult site to work on. Everyone needed to take care. The local volunteers, some of whom enjoyed fishing here, were very motivated because of the concern about the river washing these banks away.
