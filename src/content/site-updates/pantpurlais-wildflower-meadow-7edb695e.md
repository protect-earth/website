Spring or early autumn are the best time to plant a wildflower meadow, and a wind-free day is essential if you want the seed in the right place. Warm, wet conditions are ideal, so we aimed for late April just before rain was forecast to give the seed the best chance of germinating successfully.

The soil here is fertile, which isn’t ideal. The one-acre field was ploughed and power-harrowed to prepare for the seed. We prepared the wildflower seed mix by adding sand to make it easier to sow evenly and to help reduce the fertility of the soil a little. We then sowed the seed broadcast and rolled it in afterwards.

In the first year the wildflower meadow will need cutting in mid-July and the cut material removed to reduce soil fertility. Tall plants such as  docks, thistles, and nettles may need cutting earlier before they have time to seed.  The whole meadow will need cutting again in the autumn.
